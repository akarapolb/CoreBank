CoreBank: AI-native core banking platform

The reference design for a core banking platform serving retail, commercial and virtual banks, with any loan, lease or hire-purchase product built from configuration. It covers what the system does, how it is structured, where it runs, how it is protected and which rules it satisfies.

Version0.1 draft
Date13 Oct 2026
StatusFor review
Launch marketThailand (assumed)

1Introduction

This document is the single reference for design decisions on CoreBank. Detailed reasoning for individual decisions lives in the architecture decision records (ADRs) in the repository under docs/adr/; this document summarises them and shows how they fit together.

Scope

In scope

  • Customer (party) master and KYC status
  • Deposits, loans, credit lines, leases, hire purchase
  • Multi-currency double-entry ledger and GL
  • Product engine, pricing, fees, tax
  • Limits, collateral, covenants
  • Collections, TFRS 9 and TFRS 16 accounting
  • AI agent layer and AI decisioning

Integrated, not built

  • Card processing and card switch
  • Payment scheme connectivity
  • Identity verification vendors
  • CRM and marketing automation
  • Treasury front office

Audience

  • Engineering and architecture
  • Product and operations
  • Risk, compliance and audit
  • Bank IT governance and the regulator, as supporting material

Design principles

#PrincipleWhat it means in practice
P1The ledger is sacredEvery movement of value is a balanced, immutable posting. Corrections are reversals. Balances can always be rebuilt from postings.
P2Products are dataA product is a versioned definition, simulated and approved before use. New products do not need a release.
P3Bitemporal by defaultEvery fact has a value date and a booking time. Back-valued events are replayed and adjusted, never rewritten.
P4No batch windowAccrual, billing and aging run per account on durable timers. End of day is a reporting cut, not an outage.
P5API-first, agent-firstEvery capability is an API; the same contracts are AI tools. No back doors.
P6AI proposes, policy disposesAgents act through the same entitlement, limit and approval checks as people, and every action is logged.
P7Open source, bank-compatibleOnly OSI-licensed components; every infrastructure dependency can be swapped for the bank's approved equivalent.
P8Deterministic and replayableSame events and product version give the same balances to the satang. This underpins simulation, reconciliation and audit.

Commercial drawdown

Request→Covenant and limit check (atomic)→Collateral cover check→Approval per authority→Post drawdown→Pay out

3.2Modules and dependency rules

  1. *-domain imports no Spring, MyBatis, Kafka or JDBC.
  2. Dependencies point inward: adapters → application → domain.
  3. A service reaches another only through its *-api module.
  4. engines/* are pure: no clock, no I/O.
  5. ledger imports no engine and no product service.
  6. platform-money bans float and double.
  7. Database-vendor SQL lives only under postgresql/ and oracle/ in persistence adapters.

4.2The posting path

4.3Data architecture

Ledger model

One ledger account per arrangement holds many balance types (principal, interest due, VAT due, penalty, suspense). Amounts are integer minor units; no floating point anywhere.

Bitemporal

Postings carry value date and booking time. Balances are queryable as of either. Back-valuation replays the contract and posts today's adjustment.

Events

Every state change is an event with a versioned schema, landed in an Iceberg lakehouse for reporting, features and audit.

StoreHoldsTechnologyConsistency
Ledger shardsPostings, balances, holdsPostgreSQL (default) or Oracle, sharded by accountStrong, synchronous replicas across zones
Service databasesContracts, products, parties, limitsPostgreSQL or Oracle, one schema per serviceStrong within a service
Event logDomain eventsKafka, 3 zones, replication factor 3Ordered per key, replayable
Cache and online featuresReference data, model featuresValkeyRebuildable; never holds money
LakehouseHistory, reporting, training dataIceberg on object storage, Trino, dbt CoreEventual, point-in-time correct
PII vaultNational IDs, contact detailsTokenization service backed by OpenBaoStrong; tokens elsewhere

AI runs in two forms. Decisioning models (credit, fraud, AML, collections propensity) are served from a model registry with monitored drift and a rule-based fallback. Agents (customer assistant, ops copilot, RM copilot, product copilot) call MCP tools through the agent gateway, which checks delegated authority and policy before every write and records the prompt, tools, model version and outcome. Models are self-hosted open-weight models by default, so customer data stays inside the bank.

5.3Delivery pipeline

Commit→Build and unit tests→ArchUnit rules→SAST · secrets · SQL lint→SBOM and licence gate→PostgreSQL + Oracle tests→Signed image→SIT→Performance gate→UAT→Canary to production

Infrastructure is code (OpenTofu, Helm). Argo CD applies every change from Git, so production state is always reviewable and reversible. Images are signed and carry provenance; clusters admit only signed images.

5.4Resilience and recovery

  • Three availability zones; services spread across them with pod disruption budgets.
  • Ledger databases: synchronous replica in another zone, automatic failover, fencing of the old primary.
  • Backups: continuous WAL archiving plus daily full backups, immutable copies in the DR region, monthly restore tests.
  • Chaos tests on the posting path every release: kill a primary, partition a zone, slow Kafka.
  • Graceful degradation: if fraud scoring times out, the rule-based fallback decides; if Kafka is unavailable, the outbox buffers and postings continue.

6Security

Security follows zero trust: every call is authenticated and authorized, every link is encrypted, and no network location is trusted by default.

6.1Identity and access

PrincipalAuthenticationAuthorization
CustomerOIDC with device binding; step-up biometric or OTP for payments and changesOwns their arrangements only; consent scopes
StaffBank SSO with MFA, zero-trust network accessRoles plus attributes (branch, segment, amount); maker-checker on sensitive actions
ServiceWorkload identity, mesh mTLS certificatesAllow-listed service-to-service calls
AI agentAgent identity plus the human or customer it acts forPer-tool grants and amount limits; above limit, escalate to a person
PartnerOAuth2 client credentials with mTLSScoped APIs, rate limits

Maker-checker applies to product publishing, GL mapping changes, manual journals, limit overrides, fee waivers above threshold, write-offs and agent actions above authority. The checker can never be the maker.

6.2Data protection

  • TLS 1.3 in transit everywhere; mTLS inside the mesh.
  • Encryption at rest with per-tenant keys; master keys in the bank's HSM.
  • National ID, phone and address tokenized in the PII vault; services and logs see tokens only.
  • Masked data in all non-production environments.
  • Data classification on every field drives masking, retention and what AI tools may return.

6.3AI security

RiskControl
Prompt injection through customer messages or documentsUntrusted content is marked as data; tool calls are checked by policy, not by the model's judgement

Wrong or harmful adviceAnswers cite product terms; evaluation suites and red-teaming before each release

6.4Threat model: posting path

Threat (STRIDE)ExampleMitigation
SpoofingA service impersonates lending to post moneymTLS workload identity; ledger accepts only allow-listed callers per account type
TamperingA posting is edited after commitNo update or delete grants on posting tables; reversals only; hash-chained audit
RepudiationNobody owns a manual journalActor, on-behalf-of, reason and trace id on every batch
Information disclosureBalances leak through logsStructured logging with masking; account numbers tokenized
Denial of serviceRetry storm on PostBatchIdempotency keys, rate limits, bulkheads per cell
Elevation of privilegeAn agent posts above its limitAuthority checked at the gateway and again by policy at the service

6.5Detection and response

  • Security events, audit logs and agent actions stream to the SIEM; alerts on privilege changes, unusual postings and failed approvals.
  • Secure SDLC: threat modelling per service, SAST, DAST, dependency scanning, secret scanning, signed builds.
  • Penetration test before launch and yearly; bug bounty after launch.
  • Incident runbooks with regulator notification timelines; quarterly tabletop exercises.

7.3Model risk management

  • Model inventory with owner, purpose, risk tier, data lineage and approval status.
  • Independent validation, challenger models, and fairness testing on protected attributes.
  • Reason codes for every adverse credit decision, stored with the decision.
  • Monitoring for drift and stability; automatic fallback to rules on breach.
  • For agents: evaluation suites, red-teaming, versioned prompts and a kill switch per agent.

9Glossary

TermMeaning
ArrangementA customer contract: deposit, loan, lease or hire purchase.
Balance typeA named bucket inside a ledger account, such as principal or VAT due.
BitemporalRecording both when something is effective (value date) and when it was recorded (booking time).
CELCommon Expression Language: safe, non-looping formulas used in product definitions.
CellA complete copy of the stack serving a group of tenants.
DPDDays past due.
ECLExpected credit loss under TFRS 9.
EIREffective interest rate, used for income recognition and disclosure.
Flat rateInterest calculated on the original principal for the whole term, common in hire purchase.
MCPModel Context Protocol: the standard through which AI agents call tools.
Maker-checkerA second person approves what the first person prepared.
OutboxA table written in the same transaction as business data, from which events are published.
SagaA multi-step process where each step has a compensating action if a later step fails.
